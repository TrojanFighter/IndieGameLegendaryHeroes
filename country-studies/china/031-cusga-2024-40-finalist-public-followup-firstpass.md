Unlike winner/media-based case selection, this round began from all 40 project-title + team pairs in the reconstructed finalist roster and issued one public search `"<title>" "<public team alias>" [game/2025/2026]` per baseline entry in category batches, then cross-checked identifiable leads against Steam primary storefront, itch project, official studio self-publishing, developer's public 2024 documentation and contemporaneous alumni interviews. This is **FIRST_PASS_DISCOVERY** and not exhaustive result verification for all 40 across Steam/itch/GmHub/TapTap/B站; search coverage and ambiguity are asymmetric. Search engines favor store pages and featured games.

**What can already be stated at this evidence threshold:**
- **8/40** have high-confidence original-project Steam records, not necessarily new, completed or released after the contest: F03 F08 F12 F13 F15 F23 F34 F39.
- **2/40** have Steam titles and similar gameplay, but original public team aliases do not directly align (F09 F32): `ALIAS_RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED` and **excluded from strict 8** until official developer lineage is established.
- **1/40** has itch downloadable Windows/macOS evidence, with creator continuity through original published production credits (F33): it is public distribution/continued build, not a Steam commercial sale.
- **2 of those 8 Steam projects were already released *before* T0**: F12《彼方的她》（2024-04-25）and F39《随机军团》（2024-05-13）. Therefore treating all current released projects as “after-finals conversion” is **retroactive attribution**.

- 1 2024 competing project F02 has confirmed *partial alumni creator branching* into a distinct project, but original game release remains UNVERIFIED, and most members' futures UNKNOWN.

## 3. First-pass bias and what it invalidates

- Steam store discovery far outperforms `GmHub`, Bilibili small uploads and nonindexed itch under the current search approach; apparent “published globally” share is a **visibility lower bound**, not an intention measure.
- Already released games enter **student competitions**. Such competitions select quality and maturity, and later games may have been invested in long before nomination. “Award-to-launch success rate” needs T0 before first market access, or at least competing risks/left truncation.
- Award *prize* compared with student submission is a selection filter; 40 finalists are too selected to estimate ordinary student innovation awareness. Even follow-up complete 40/40 will only describe this **particular selected finalist cohort**.
- A studio can have one successful marketed game and other team members can work elsewhere; don't collapse game project, team and named creator into one longitudinal entity.
- Paid release, free finished release, downloadable prototype, Demo, store page without date, company formation and investment are **distinct evidence gates**.
- CUSGA 2024 has nonmainland eligible participants; without each baseline's region, even the selected 40 project cross-region “China rate” is not yet defensible.
