## E001 — PC Gamer 2016 creator interview: solo work and learning

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview immediately after breakout
- Source: PC Gamer, `Interview: What's next for Stardew Valley`, 2016
- URL: https://www.pcgamer.com/stardew-valley-interview/

Eric Barone states:
- he made art, sound, music, code and the rest of the game himself;
- development lasted about four years;
- it began as a Harvest Moon-like project partly to teach himself C#;
- because he initially had little experience, he redid nearly everything multiple times.

Supports: C003, C006, C011.

## E002 — PC Gamer 2016 creator interview: life runway

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: same interview, page 2
- URL: https://www.pcgamer.com/stardew-valley-interview/2/

Barone states:
- he worked part-time as a theater usher;
- he lived with his girlfriend and they were largely living on her graduate-school stipend / income;
- friends/family generally believed in him.

Supports:
- C002/C001: non-project household resources helped make a long development window survivable.

Boundary:
- does not justify reducing the creative labor credit of the solo developer.

## E003 — ConcernedApe 2019 official clarification

- Class: P1 — creator-owned official statement
- Source: Eric Barone, `Clarification on the relationship between Chucklefish and Stardew Valley`, 2019-09-14
- URL: https://www.stardewvalley.net/clarification-on-the-relationship-between-chucklefish-and-stardew-valley/

Barone states:
- throughout 4.5 years of development he was the only person working on the game;
- Chucklefish did not create the core game;
- multiplayer netcode was later written by a Chucklefish employee after launch;
- publisher work covered business/marketing and contracting localizations and console/mobile ports.

Supports:
- strong solo-core classification;
- contributor audit must distinguish game-content authorship from business/porting/localization labor.

## E004 — Chucklefish 2022 retrospective

- Class: P1 — publisher retrospective
- Source: Chucklefish, `A thank you to Stardew Valley`, 2022
- URL: https://chucklefish.org/blog/thank-you-stardew-valley/

Publisher states:
- team discovered an early Stardew Valley on Steam Greenlight in 2014;
- agreement to work together followed in 2015;
- the game already existed in meaningful form before publisher partnership.

Supports:
- publisher was an amplifier/periphery, not the origin of the game.

## E005 — ConcernedApe 2018 self-publishing announcement

- Class: P1 — creator-owned official statement
- Source: Eric Barone, `Move to Self-Publishing starting December 14th`, 2018
- URL: https://www.stardewvalley.net/move-to-self-publishing-starting-december-14th/

Barone credits Chucklefish with:
- distribution;
- console ports and translations oversight;
- official wiki;
- website redesign;
- later network code.

Supports:
- C010: market/business infrastructure existed even when core creation was solo.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- pre-launch core product was genuinely solo.
- four-plus years of capability acquisition/rework occurred inside the project.
- partner income and part-time work materially supported life runway.
- publisher/business/porting/localization perimeter existed without negating solo core authorship.

### UNKNOWN
- precise household burn and funding mix;
- publisher contract/marketing spend;
- causal role of Greenlight/community relative to launch word of mouth.
