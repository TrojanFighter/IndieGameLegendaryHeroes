## E001 — Innersloth official press kit

- Class: P1 — official creator retrospective
- Source: Innersloth, `Press Kit Among Us`
- URL: https://www.innersloth.com/press-kit-among-us/

Official facts:
- mobile release in 2018;
- original production by three people at the time: Forest, Marcus, and later Amy for additional art;
- team kept improving maps, online play and cosmetics;
- game was considered complete in January 2020 before worldwide popularity in summer 2020.

Direct quotes (verbatim, page re-read 2026-10-09):

Design origin:
- "Among Us was cofounder Marcus Bromander's idea based on a combination of a Mafia-like game he played as a kid and the movie 'The Thing'. In the original game, you would draw a role card and roam around a house aimlessly while someone secretly 'killed' players by drawing a finger across [their back]…"

Team size:
- "made by only 3 people at the time – Forest, Marcus, and (later on) Amy for additional art."

Scale by late 2020:
- "In November 2020, Among Us had almost 500 million monthly active users. Garnering more than 4 billion views on YouTube by Oct 2020, it also had more than 1.22 billion viewing sessions on Twitch in Q4 2020."

Supports:
- C003/C011: small team and multi-year iteration precede breakout.
Additional boundary:
- The 2020 figures are studio-stated platform metrics, not audited.
- The design-origin story is the studio's own retrospective; "The Thing" and the childhood Mafia game are named as inspirations, not as documented sources of specific mechanics.

## E002 — PC and Online Released, 2018-08-17

- Class: P0 — contemporaneous primary devlog
- Source: Innersloth, `PC and Online Released!`
- URL: https://www.innersloth.com/pc-and-online-released/

Source-derived facts:
- mobile beta had launched in June;
- netcode was overhauled for online play;
- scope expanded to a PC release;
- Discord/Twitter were already feedback channels.

Direct quote (verbatim, page re-read 2026-10-09):
- "We released Among Us to beta for Android and iOS in June. In the last few months, we have overhauled the netcode for much greater reliability (Still in beta, though. Let us know about them bugs!) and we are now capable of online play! And since we were scope-creeping anyway, we saw no reason not to [release on PC]."

Supports:
- C004/C010: technical and market infrastructure evolved together.
Additional boundary:
- "we were scope-creeping anyway" is the team's own self-description, not evidence that the PC expansion was a mistake.

## E003 — The Data Among Us, 2018-10-04

- Class: P0 — contemporaneous primary devlog
- Source: Innersloth, `The Data Among Us`
- URL: https://www.innersloth.com/the-data-among-us/

Source-derived facts:
- team collected opt-in gameplay telemetry;
- dataset already represented tens of thousands of games/submissions;
- itch.io retweet corresponded with a visible growth spike.

Direct quotes (verbatim, page re-read 2026-10-09):
- "This isn't quite a post-mortem, although we are planning on wrapping Among Us up soon to make way for another game."
- On the data volume: "we're sitting on over 15000 games worth of data across >65000 submissions taking 1.2GB."
- On where play actually happened: "the vast majority of games are played locally with friends. To be honest, that's exactly how it's meant to be."
- On a server outage: "The dip on October 1st? That's the server outage when I messed up releasing 2018.9.30. (Sorry!)"

Supports:
- feedback/analytics and discoverability existed well before 2020.
Additional boundary:
- Written 2018-10-04: the team was already describing the game as near its end more than a year and a half before the 2020 breakout. This is the earliest endpoint statement in the record.

## E004 — Among Us and Innersloth in 2020, 2020-01-06

- Class: P0 — contemporaneous primary devlog
- Source: Innersloth, `Among Us and Innersloth in 2020`
- URL: https://www.innersloth.com/among-us-and-innersloth-in-2020/amp/

Team states:
- they had considered the game complete more than once;
- after Polus they again considered it complete and planned only limited bugfixes;
- purchases would help keep servers running and fund new games.

Direct quotes (verbatim, page re-read 2026-10-09):
- "We thought it was a completed game twice in the past as we came back to add online multiplayer and two new maps. But as of the Polus release, we're sure that Among Us is a complete game."
- "All purchases made in Among Us will go towards keeping the servers running and us making new games, just like always."
- On what the studio planned instead: "Once The Henry Stickmin Collection and Monty's Halls come out, hopefully we'll be in a good position to take a nice vacation, then start on a bigger game all three of us together."

Supports:
- breakout happened after the team's own production endpoint.
- product revenue was part of studio runway.
Additional boundary:
- Dated 2020-01-06. The worldwide breakout came months later, so this is a pre-breakout statement of an endpoint, not a retrospective account of one.

## E005 — The Future of Among Us, 2020-09-24

- Class: P0 — contemporaneous primary devlog
- Source: Innersloth, `The Future of Among Us`
- URL: https://www.innersloth.com/new-hotfixes-are-live-2-3/

Team states:
- Among Us 2 was cancelled after the unexpected popularity of the original;
- planned sequel content would instead go into the first game;
- this was harder because the old codebase had not been built for the new scale/content.

Direct quotes (verbatim, page re-read 2026-10-09):
- "The main reason we are shooting for a sequel is because the codebase of Among Us 1 is so outdated and not built to support adding so much new content." (written before the sequel was cancelled)
- On the server load: "Forte is still working very hard to figure out our server issues. We don't have an ETA but things should hopefully be getting better and better. This is taking up all development time on his end."
- On colorblind support: "It's something we've put off for way too long… We plan on adding other identifiers for players as well as certain color focused tasks."

Supports:
- success can create new production debt rather than ending production problems.
Additional boundary:
- The quoted sequel rationale was written days before the cancellation was announced; the two statements must be read together rather than as a contradiction.

## E006 — 2021 organization / partner restructuring explanation

- Class: P1/P0 — official creator explanation close to event
- Source: Innersloth, `The upcoming Airship map, consoles, and what's next`, 2021
- URL: https://www.innersloth.com/the-upcoming-airship-map-consoles-and-whats-next-%F0%9F%8E%88/

Official explanation:
- team had to return to a game they thought was done;
- roughly two months were spent restructuring processes;
- external partners were brought in to make the organization sustainable for long-term work and hiring.

Direct quote (verbatim, page re-read 2026-10-09):
- "Happy 2021! Here's a giant dev log update, a wrap up, and mini roadmap for the time ahead."

Boundary:
- The fetched copy of this page carried only its opening; the restructuring detail in the summary above is not quoted here and should be re-read from the page before being used as a quotation.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

- 2020 breakout was late relative to release/development;
- the game had years of iteration and existing feedback channels;
- team considered it complete before the market explosion;
- success forced technical and organizational restructuring.

### UNKNOWN

- channel attribution for global breakout;
- complete external partner/contributor map.
