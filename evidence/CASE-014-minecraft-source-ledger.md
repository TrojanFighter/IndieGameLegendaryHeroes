# Evidence Ledger — CASE-014 Minecraft / Markus Persson → Mojang

- Status: ACTIVE

## E001 — Wired profile: early alpha, TIGSource and weekly iteration

- Class: S1/P1 — quality secondary with direct creator interview
- Source: Wired, `Changing the game: how Notch made Minecraft a cult hit`
- URL: https://www.wired.com/story/changing-the-game/

Source-derived facts:
- Persson produced a basic early Minecraft build over a weekend in May 2009 and mentioned it on TIGSource.
- Roughly a month later he began charging for access.
- Wired reports 40 copies sold in the first paid weekend.
- Persson kept engaging early players and shipped frequent updates; the profile describes sales rising alongside updates and word of mouth.

Supports:
- C010: audience formation, feedback and distribution began during development.
- C004: an incomplete but playable product became part of the production system.

Does not support:
- that the entire game was made in one weekend;
- that word of mouth alone explains eventual scale.

## E002 — Minecraft book excerpt / Wired: release early, charge early, quit threshold

- Class: P1/S1 — retrospective creator quotations embedded in reported history
- Source: Wired, `The Amazingly Unlikely Story of How Minecraft Was Born`, 2013
- URL: https://www.wired.com/2013/11/minecraft-book/

Source-derived facts:
- first public TIGSource version is dated 2009-05-17;
- Persson described an explicit staged pricing plan during development;
- paid orders opened in June 2009;
- Persson later said early release and charging helped force completion and establish value;
- he calculated a daily-sales threshold at which he could leave his day job.

Supports:
- C002: recurring product revenue can become runway.
- C004: release/pricing design can manufacture production conditions.
- C010: market validation was not post-production.

Boundary:
- reported early sales figures are not a full audited budget.

## E003 — Game Developer interview on alpha funding

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: Game Developer, `When players buy your game before it's done: Expert tips on alpha funding`, 2013
- URL: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/when-players-buy-your-game-before-it-s-done-expert-tips-on-alpha-funding

Persson's position:
- Minecraft was a good fit for releasing/funding early;
- the same approach does not fit every project, especially some story-heavy games.

Supports:
- the financing method was structurally matched to the product, not a universal recipe.

## E004 — PC Gamer reconstruction of the first TIGSource post

- Class: S1 — archival secondary
- Source: PC Gamer, `The first moments of Minecraft`, 2011
- URL: https://www.pcgamer.com/the-first-moments-of-minecraft/

Source-derived facts:
- preserves the May 17, 2009 TIGSource posting time and Persson's early description citing Infiniminer and a Dwarf Fortress direction.

Supports:
- early public provenance and inspiration chain.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- the famous weekend refers to an early public core, not a finished product;
- Minecraft entered public feedback and paid access extremely early;
- early sales influenced Persson's ability to treat development as a job;
- alpha funding and community iteration were part of production, not merely promotion.

### UNKNOWN
- complete pre-2009 capability timeline;
- exact net income/runway before leaving paid employment;
- precise transition point from solo project to Mojang organization;
- causal weight of specific channels in later explosive growth.
