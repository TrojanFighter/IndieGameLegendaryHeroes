- Source class: P0 — creator's own retrospective.
- Title: Queen's Wish: A Portmortem Of Mixed Success.
- Author / Institution: Jeff Vogel / The Bottom Feeder.
- Published: 2026-03-11.
- Accessed: 2026-10-07.
- URL: https://bottomfeeder.substack.com/p/queens-wish-a-portmortem-of-mixed
- Claim use: Vogel says QW1 sold well, QW2 sold roughly half as much on Steam and underperformed Spiderweb history; full third game became infeasible, remasters restored business stability, and a smaller free epilogue finished the story. - Confidence: VERY HIGH.
- Boundary: creator-reported sales without unit totals; marketer failure, visuals and indie glut are also named causes, so do not attribute underperformance solely to FIT-LOCK-IN.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- Spiderweb has deliberately reused engines/assets and a narrow RPG grammar for decades as cost control.
- Vogel explicitly links niche fit to company survival and family-support risk.
- audience resistance to change is a long-observed creator-reported constraint.
- familiar technology and grammar support faster production; a new engine/system significantly increases time and uncertainty.
- Queen's Wish was a deliberate large transition within the RPG domain.
- QW1 eventually found an audience, but QW2 materially underperformed QW1 and changed the planned trilogy scope.
- Vogel explicitly interprets the transition problem as needing new customers to replace old customers lost through design change.

### SUPPORTED WITH BOUNDARY
- Spiderweb is a strong second `FIT-LOCK-IN` anchor after Zachtronics.
- long-run fit compounds not just capability but technology, assets, audience expectations, brand and cashflow forecasts.
- changing grammar therefore carries simultaneous production and market switching costs.

### UNKNOWN / DO NOT INFER
- exact audited annual profits/revenue;
- exact QW1/QW2 units across all platforms;
- causal share of design change vs marketing/graphics/market glut in QW2;
- whether a different genre would perform worse;
- complete household finances;
- whether all veteran niche studios develop comparable lock-in.
