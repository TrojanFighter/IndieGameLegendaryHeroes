## E001 — Microsoft Developer Acceleration Program Spotlight

- Class: P1/P0 — platform retrospective with direct developer interview
- Source: Microsoft Game Dev
- Interviewee: Julián Cordero
- Publication date: 2025-03-06
- URL: https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/games/articles/2025/03/developer-acceleration-program-spotlight-julian-cordero/

Source-derived facts:
- Cordero says he had known and admired Sebastián Valbuena's art/music through Valbuena's brother, and brought him onto despelote despite Valbuena having no prior videogame production experience;
- the team later added Ian Berman for sound and Gabe Cuzzillo for production support;
- despelote participated in the NYU Game Center Incubator, where Cordero met industry advisors including Sarah Conde from ID@Xbox;
- Conde told the team about DAP; they applied and received a grant;
- critically, Cordero says development money had run out and the team was taking freelance work to pay bills;
- the DAP grant let them return to full-time development and prepare a publisher-pitch vertical slice;
- Cordero explicitly says that publisher pitch was ultimately successful and credits the DAP bridge as necessary for that chain;
- he also says early prototypes connected strongly with people, which encouraged the team to become ambitious before understanding the production cost, and that completing the final package took far longer than expected.

Supports:
- C002: a relatively small/phase-specific grant can be valuable by restoring full-time runway rather than by covering the entire budget;
- C004: the team converted a grant into a specific next-stage financing asset (vertical slice);
- C010: incubator/advisor/publisher relationships are part of production and financing, not post-hoc marketing;
- C011: visible production duration and “two creators” framing omit a long sequence of institutional and professional support.

Boundary:
- Microsoft has promotional incentives around DAP;
- grant amount and exact payment date are not disclosed;
- the interview establishes sequence and developer interpretation, but not the contents of the eventual Panic contract.

## E007 — Unity development interview with Julián Cordero

- Class: P1 — developer retrospective via engine vendor
- Source: Unity
- Publication date: 2025-06-11
- URL: https://unity.com/blog/despelote-creator-narrative-game-design-goals

Source-derived facts:
- Cordero began the project as designer/programmer and brought in Valbuena for art/music despite Valbuena having no 3D/game background;
- first months centered on the physical pleasure of kicking the ball;
- the team later discovered overheard conversations as an important way to absorb the world;
- conventional objectives/task lists conflicted with the intended childhood experience;
- a time/watch mechanic became a major structural breakthrough because the game could progress regardless of player location;
- visual production combined 3D scans/photographic capture from Quito with 2D hand-drawn style and custom shader treatment.

Supports:
- C004: the team redefined representation and narrative structure to fit a small author's production system;
- C011: a strong early mechanic did not mean the game was structurally solved; much of development was prolonged design search.

Boundary:
- Unity has promotional incentives to foreground engine-enabled solutions;
- retrospective “breakthrough” language cannot alone quantify how many months/years were saved or spent.

## E008 — post-release development/funding retrospectives

- Class: P1/S1 — direct developer quotes in edited press
- Sources:
  - CNN Español syndicated reporting, 2025-06-16: https://www.wral.com/archive/22052849/
  - El Telégrafo interview: https://www.eltelegrafo.com.ec/noticias/tendencias/213/juego-despelote-mundial-videojuego-ecuador
  - Mundo Diners interview with Sebastián Valbuena, 2026-04-26: https://revistamundodiners.com/reportaje/tecnologia/despelote-videojuego-ecuatoriano/

Source-derived facts:
- Cordero dates project start to summer 2018 and describes only roughly the final three years as full-time; before that it was part-time;
- he says the project was initially expected to take no more than about a year but instead took roughly seven;
- he describes funding sequence as Microsoft support, Ecuador cultural funding, and ultimately Panic, with Panic enabling the team to work full-time;
- Cordero says it took about five years to understand the final narrative arc and how mechanics/world fit together;
- Valbuena says Ecuador public funding was USD 10,000 and represented less than 10% of total investment; he says the project would not have been completed without Panic financing;
- El Telégrafo says Panic also handled advertising/marketing and ports to multiple platforms.

Supports:
- C002: funding arrived as a sequence of bridges rather than one complete budget;
- C004: publisher financing changed the team's feasible work mode;
- C010: publisher value included distribution/marketing/ports, not just cash;
- C011: initial one-year schedule badly understated the real design/production search.

Boundary:
- post-release recollection can round dates and time allocation;
- “less than 10%” should not be inverted into a precise budget estimate;
- press summaries may collapse Microsoft DAP and other Microsoft/Xbox support into a single label.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- despelote began as a student/thesis-era project and had years of institutional, public-showcase and prototype history before release;
- the project experienced an explicit cash-runway failure point that forced freelance work;
- Microsoft DAP funding restored full-time development and was used to make a publisher-pitch vertical slice;
- Cordero says that pitch succeeded, and Panic subsequently financed/published the project;
- Ecuador IFCI officially awarded despelote USD 10,000 in 2021;
- public grant money was one layer, not the total budget;
- full release production extended well beyond the two named creators;
- the seven-year timeline contained long design convergence, not just linear content execution.

### SUPPORTED WITH BOUNDARY
- NYU Incubator advisor access was a material bridge to DAP because the developer explicitly identifies that environment as where the relevant ID@Xbox contact was made;
- Panic financing was necessary to sustain the final full-time phase according to developer retrospectives;
- cultural specificity helped differentiation in pitches/funding, but its causal weight cannot be isolated from prototype quality and networks.

### UNKNOWN
- exact DAP amount and timing;
- exact NYU Incubator stipend to despelote;
- Panic contract economics and signing date;
- household/family/savings support across seven years;
- total budget under a consistent accounting definition;
- failed grant recipients / incubator alumni needed for a proper counterfactual success-rate comparison.
