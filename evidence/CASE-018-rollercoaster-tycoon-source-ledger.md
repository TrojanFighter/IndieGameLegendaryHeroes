- RollerCoaster Tycoon 2 followed in 2002 through Infogrames Interactive.

Supports:
- C003/C011: RCT rests on long technical/commercial prehistory;
- the OPC production pattern persisted across more than one commercial release.

## E005 — later direct interview on assembler choice

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: Atari Club / CarlyF, `Interview with RollerCoaster Tycoon’s Creator, Chris Sawyer`
- URL: https://medium.com/atari-club/interview-with-rollercoaster-tycoons-creator-chris-sawyer-684a0efb0f13

Sawyer explains:
- contemporary PC performance made highly optimized machine code important for his desired simulation density and frame rate;
- assembler was also the language in which he personally worked fastest and most reliably;
- later mobile reimplementation in C++ required a small team and years, illustrating the path dependence of his original choice.

Boundary:
- does not prove assembler is generally superior; the claim is specific to Sawyer's skills and hardware context.

## E007 — Sawyer on the shared Transport Tycoon code lineage

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: GameSpot, `RollerCoaster Tycoon designer offers first details on new title`, 2004-04-05
- URL: https://www.gamespot.com/articles/rollercoaster-tycoon-designer-offers-first-details-on-new-title/1100-6092788/

Sawyer states that he had been working on new Transport Tycoon versions on and off since 1996, and that **both RollerCoaster Tycoon games grew out of code written specifically for a new Transport Tycoon version**.

Supports:
- C004/C011: RCT1 and RCT2 share a deeper code/idea lineage with repeated abandoned/postponed transport-game attempts;
- the economically relevant unit is accumulated code/tool/capability capital, not a fresh isolated project.

## E008 — RCT2 and the deliberate refusal of a large graphical upgrade

- Class: P0/S1 — contemporaneous direct creator quotation preserved by a secondary outlet
- Source: HomeLAN interview with Chris Sawyer, excerpt preserved by GGMania, `RollerCoaster Tycoon II Interview`, 2002-06-27
- URL: https://www.ggmania.com/?smsid=rollercoaster-tycoon-ii-interview-11576

Sawyer explains that he could have spent several years on a major graphical upgrade, but questioned whether that would make the game more fun; he prioritized interaction, construction and gameplay instead.

Supports:
- C007: scope control operates at the technology/representation layer, not only at the individual-feature layer;
- a sequel can preserve an older visual architecture when the opportunity cost of replacing it is too high.

Boundary:
- the surviving source is an excerpt/secondary preservation of the direct interview. Seek the original HomeLAN/GameSpy-era interview archive before using exact wording in reader-facing prose.

## E009 — later Sawyer interview on RCT2 micro-detail and design continuity

- Class: P1 — direct creator retrospective
- Source: Arcade Attack, `Chris Sawyer (Transport Tycoon/RollerCoaster Tycoon) - Interview`, 2018
- URL: https://www.arcadeattack.co.uk/chris-sawyer-interview/

Sawyer describes a last-minute RCT2 guest reaction feature implemented with only a few lines of code and a few animation frames, and reflects on the difficulty of modernizing RCT without losing its playability.

Supports:
- small, low-cost details could be preferred over prestige-heavy feature work when they had high experiential value;
- continuity of design judgment matters alongside technical capability.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
