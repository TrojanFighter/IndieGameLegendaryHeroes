## E001 — Unity case study: Escape from Tarkov / Battlestate Games

- Class: P1/P0 — platform case study with direct developer quotation
- Source: Unity, Made with Unity — Escape from Tarkov
- URL: https://unity.com/made-with-unity/escape-from-tarkov

Source-derived facts:
- Unity describes Battlestate's foundational team as having started at AbsolutSoft making the browser / Unity Web Player FPS Contract Wars;
- Unity states that Contract Wars was a training ground for new skills and expertise while also building finances needed to evolve and found Battlestate Games;
- Nikita Buyanov says the AbsolutSoft experience produced critical FPS knowledge and deep understanding of Unity and its systems;
- he explicitly says that this gave Escape from Tarkov a strong design and technology foundation.

Supports:
- C002: prior product revenue created real runway for the next, riskier project;
- C004: the team manufactured its own production conditions before attempting EFT;
- C006: the capability path came through a smaller professional studio/product lineage rather than requiring AAA pedigree;
- C011: EFT's visible production period omits a material Contract Wars capability prehistory.

Boundary:
- Unity has a promotional interest in emphasizing its engine;
- the page does not disclose exact Contract Wars revenue, capital transferred to Battlestate, or which individual developers moved.

## E002 — Nikita Buyanov interview with Dmitry Puchkov / Goblin

- Class: P1 — direct developer retrospective interview
- Source: Tynu40k Goblina / oper.ru
- Interviewee: Nikita Buyanov
- URL: https://oper.ru/video/view.php?t=1994

Source-derived facts:
- Buyanov says the team first made Contract Wars, a browser shooter;
- he describes its purpose as testing technology, understanding what the team could make, and earning money to continue working;
- he says they had no investor and the project/funds were their own.

Supports:
- C002 / C004: Contract Wars functioned as a deliberate capability-and-runway staging project;
- C011: EFT should not be treated as a from-zero startup story.

Boundary:
- retrospective self-report may simplify legal/corporate financing arrangements;
- “no investor” should not be generalized to “no outside commercial relationships” without further company records.

## E003 — GameSpot 2016 Nikita Buyanov Q&A

- Class: P0/P1 — contemporaneous direct developer interview during early development
- Source: GameSpot, Ivan Ho
- Publication date: 2016-03-29
- URL: https://www.gamespot.com/articles/how-modding-microtransactions-and-death-will-work-/1100-6436115/

Source-derived facts:
- Buyanov describes EFT as intentionally difficult and reality-oriented rather than optimized for conventional FPS fun;
- he says the team would test demanding mechanics on themselves and Alpha/Beta players;
- he explicitly states EFT would be a traditional full purchase rather than free-to-play or microtransaction-driven.

Supports:
- C004: the team used its accumulated optionality to pursue a product that rejected the most obvious monetization continuation from Contract Wars;
- C010: the commercial model itself is part of product positioning and market access;
- supports the distinction between capability reuse and product-definition reuse.

Boundary:
- this interview records intent in March 2016, not every monetization decision made over the following decade;
- ambitious planned systems should not be treated as shipped features unless separately verified.

## E005 — Escape from Tarkov preorder packages, January 2016

- Class: S1 — contemporaneous industry coverage based on official preorder information
- Source: MMOs.com
- Publication date: 2016-01-04
- URL: https://mmos.com/news/escape-from-tarkov-preorder-packages-now-available

Source-derived facts:
- four preorder packages were offered from $44.99 Standard to $139.99 Edge of Darkness;
- preorder tiers were linked to testing access and in-game starting benefits;
- the highest tier included Alpha access, while preorder generally guaranteed closed-beta access;
- the official FAQ at the time characterized the game as buy-to-play rather than F2P with microtransactions.

Supports:
- C002: preorder revenue became an additional potential runway source after Contract Wars-derived capital;
- C010: access packaging and direct preorder sales were an explicit market/financing mechanism.

Boundary:
- price tiers do not reveal sales volume, refunds, taxes or net cash available to development;
- coverage is secondary and should not be used alone for revenue estimates.

## E006 — Hired Ops interview with AbsolutSoft project leadership

- Class: P0/P1 — contemporaneous direct developer interview
- Source: MMO13, interview with Anton Shcheglov, Hired Ops development lead
- Publication date: 2016-10-27
- URL: https://mmo13.ru/news/post-2207

Source-derived facts:
- AbsolutSoft presented Hired Ops as its client shooter following the browser-era Contract Wars;
- the interview explicitly discusses systems carried from Contract Wars into Hired Ops and redesigned at a deeper level;
- the product remained centered on mercenary/PMC structures and Contract Wars-derived systems.

Supports:
- Hired Ops is best treated as an AbsolutSoft continuation branch from the same Contract Wars capability base;
- the Contract Wars production system generated more than one successor path, enabling a useful comparator to EFT.

Boundary:
- this source alone does not prove the exact corporate or IP relationship between AbsolutSoft and Battlestate;
- it must not be used to claim Hired Ops was developed by Battlestate.

## E007 — Escape from Tarkov 1.0 retrospective with Nikita Buyanov

- Class: P1 — direct developer retrospective
- Source: Wccftech
- Publication date: 2025-11-14
- URL: https://wccftech.com/escape-from-tarkov-1-0-launch-qa-we-have-big-plans/

Source-derived facts:
- immediately before 1.0, Buyanov described the journey from early-access/preorder players as more than eight years;
- he says the studio had planned to release sooner;
- he attributes the longer path to complex subsystems, obstacles, rewrites from scratch and work the team did not initially know how to execute;
- version 1.0 and the Steam launch occurred only in November 2025.

Supports:
- C011: even the internal early schedule dramatically understated actual development duration;
- C004: production optionality allowed large amounts of continued engineering and rewrite work;
- provides a counterweight to romanticizing the long beta as fully planned from the start.

Boundary:
- retrospective explanation comes from the project leader and should be cross-checked against contemporary staffing, roadmap and financial evidence;
- it does not quantify the cost of rewrites or distinguish scope expansion from unavoidable technical difficulty.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- Battlestate's foundational team had a direct professional lineage from AbsolutSoft / Contract Wars;
- Contract Wars intentionally functioned as both a technical learning project and a source of money for subsequent development;
- Contract Wars supplied FPS and Unity capability that materially lowered the starting risk of EFT;
- EFT deliberately rejected the prior project's F2P/browser product definition while reusing capability and domain knowledge;
- early EFT monetization/distribution used direct preorders and the studio's own platform before Steam;
- the final production duration greatly exceeded the team's early public schedule.

### SUPPORTED WITH BOUNDARY
- Contract Wars revenue helped make Battlestate organizational independence feasible;
- Hired Ops is a useful parallel continuation of the Contract Wars production lineage inside AbsolutSoft rather than a necessary step toward EFT;
- direct sales / preorder cash likely became a major later-stage runway source, but its magnitude still needs financial evidence.

### UNKNOWN
- exact Contract Wars revenue and how much was transferred into Battlestate;
- complete founder/employee migration map from AbsolutSoft to Battlestate;
- legal ownership/licensing of the Russia 2028 setting between the companies;
- code, asset and backend reuse across Contract Wars / Hired Ops / EFT;
- year-by-year EFT revenue, burn, headcount and external commercial support;
- quantified contribution of Twitch/YouTube and other creator amplification to major growth inflection points.
