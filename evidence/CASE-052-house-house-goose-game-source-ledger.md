## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- House House began as a friend group whose first game started as a hobby/side project.
- Untitled Goose Game had a four-person design core but a larger specialist contributor perimeter.
- Film Victoria provided separate development and completion support.
- the completion support directly enabled additional local development capability, including accessibility work.
- Panic provided production/publishing periphery beyond the core team's internal capability.
- the goose project's core comic interaction was highly legible in trailers/visual communication.
- after Goose's success, House House had substantially more self-financed risk tolerance for Big Walk.

### SUPPORTED WITH BOUNDARY
- House House is a strong grant-financed capability-expansion case.
- public grant and publisher capital bought different capability bundles and likely imposed different governance surfaces.
- outsider creative backgrounds plausibly contributed directly to visual/comedic/communication capability, but no causal ranking against traditional game training is established.

### UNKNOWN / DO NOT INFER
- exact Film Victoria grant amounts;
- exact Panic advance/revenue share/recoup/IP terms;
- total Goose budget;
- exact contributor compensation;
- precise share of Big Walk financed by Goose retained earnings;
- counterfactual outcome without public or publisher support.
