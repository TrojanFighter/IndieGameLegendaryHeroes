## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- Braid success provided founder-owned runway for a much larger second project.
- Blow deliberately rejected publisher financing he believed would compromise authorial control.
- The Witness used a small core but intentionally acquired specialized capabilities in art, architecture, landscape and other areas.
- Architecture / landscape specialists participated in iterative game-space design rather than functioning as mere cosmetic vendors.
- First-week release performance was commercially strong (>100k units, >$5m gross), though it had not yet fully recouped the long development cost at that exact moment.

### SUPPORTED WITH BOUNDARY
- The project is a strong `CAPABILITY-EXPANDED` comparator: project thesis remained larger than founder's personal skill set and missing capability was purchased/hired rather than deleted.
- It is a useful counterpressure against simplistic C014 readings: high upfront commitment can succeed under strong prior capital, reputation and thesis stability.

### UNKNOWN / DO NOT INFER
- audited lifetime net profit;
- exact break-even date;
- total all-in cost;
- full contractor/person-month map;
- counterfactual scope if Braid had earned much less;
- whether this model improves expected success relative to capability-shaped small-scope formation.
