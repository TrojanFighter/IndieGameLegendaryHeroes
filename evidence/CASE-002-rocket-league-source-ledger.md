## E001 — Guardian: overnight success was a decade in the making

- Class: P1/S1 — direct founder/design-lead interview in professional media
- Source: Keith Stuart, `Rocket League: how the game's overnight success was a decade in the making`, The Guardian, 2016-09-06
- URL: https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2016/sep/06/rocket-league-psyonix-online-sports-game-football-motor-racing-combat

Source-derived facts / direct recollection:
- Dave Hagewood says he trained Psyonix staff on Unreal Engine 3 and the company was built doing contract work for other studios.
- He describes work-for-hire as the bootstrapping mechanism while a small part of the company continued original work.
- The article traces the car/boost/ball idea through SARPBC and says the refinement process took about seven years while work-for-hire paid the bills.
- Hagewood says AAA contract work exposed Psyonix to other studios' processes and quality standards.
- Corey Davis / Hagewood discuss accessibility and team-play lessons from Battle-Cars.

Supports:
- C004: production conditions were actively manufactured.
- C005: work-for-hire can cross-subsidize original IP.
- C011: Rocket League's capability history predates its nominal two-year production window.

Boundary:
- Does not provide annual contract revenue, margin, or exact internal allocation.
- “Seven years” is a retrospective development framing, not a precise continuous full-time headcount measure.

## E002 — Los Angeles Times: budget, contract history and early scale

- Class: P1/S1 — contemporary profile with founder/company reporting
- Source: Todd Martens, `How 'Rocket League' combined cars with soccer to create video game magic`, Los Angeles Times, 2016-03-13
- URL: https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/herocomplex/la-ca-hc-theplayer-rocket-league-20160313-story.html

Reported:
- Before Rocket League, Psyonix was known largely for contract work on larger productions.
- Rocket League was developed over roughly two years with a budget just under USD 2 million.
- By the profile date it had passed 12 million players and USD 70 million revenue.

Supports:
- Rocket League's final production budget was small relative to its eventual scale.
- The company had substantial production prehistory before the two-year project window.

Boundary:
- “Budget < $2m” is not the cost of creating Psyonix's prior decade of capabilities.
- Player count is not DAU/retention; revenue is company-reported/press-reported and needs accounting context.
