## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED

- Greene entered commercial game development through modding rather than a conventional studio career ladder;
- the PLAYERUNKNOWN Battle Royale lineage predates PUBG and was publicly playable in DayZ/Arma;
- the Arma 3 mod had substantial community adoption and streamer/event visibility before Bluehole;
- H1Z1 is a licensing/consulting transition, not the origin of the mode;
- Bluehole recruited Greene after a public rule set/community already existed;
- PUBG was a commercial company/team production and must not be described as a solo indie game;
- Greene's role changed materially from mod maker to creative director/manager.

### SUPPORTED / NEEDS MORE GRANULARITY

- modding functioned as an alternative credential system: public artifacts and community behaviour made Greene legible to SOE/Bluehole before traditional game-industry credentials;
- streamer activity provided both market access and observable repeat/spectator behaviour;
- this lineage is a strong example of independently searched rule innovation being corporately amplified.

### PARTIAL / NEXT VERIFY

- exact DayZCherno+ / first Battle Royale chronology;
- collaborator/server-admin contribution boundaries in 2013–2014;
- exact design lineage of shrinking zone, plane insertion, random loot and adjacent rules;
- SOE licensing/consulting contract terms and compensation;
- Bluehole initial budget, hiring sequence and team headcount over time;
- PUBG growth decomposition across streaming, Steam discovery and regional markets;
- Brazil/Ireland runway under stronger primary documentation;
- concrete management/production failures during the modder → director transition.
