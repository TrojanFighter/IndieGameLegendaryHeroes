## E001 — ABC / triple j 2017: Greene on Brazil, nontraditional background, and relearning games

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: ABC / triple j, `Talking Battlegrounds with 'PlayerUnknown' Brendan Greene`, 2017
- URL: https://www.abc.net.au/triplej/programs/triplej-breakfast/player-unknown-iv/9099362

Useful creator testimony:
- Greene describes living in Brazil and working across photography, DJing and design-related work;
- he did not present himself as a traditional hardcore gamer or career game developer;
- DayZ / Arma reignited his interest in games;
- his route into making games came through modifying an existing playable system rather than entering a formal game-development job first.

Supports:
- nontraditional career prehistory;
- `player → tinkerer/modder → creator` transition;
- goal formation after, rather than before, an established game-industry identity.

Boundary:
- retrospective interview; use for Greene's own experience, not to infer that nontraditional backgrounds generally outperform formal training.

## E002 — PC Gamer 2015: DayZ → Arma 3 Battle Royale, player scale, and H1Z1 licensing

- Class: P1/P0-adjacent — contemporaneous direct creator interview during the H1Z1 transition
- Source: PC Gamer, `Battle Royale modder Brendan Greene on his official H1Z1 mode`, 2015-01-20
- URL: https://www.pcgamer.com/battle-royale-modder-brendan-greene-on-his-official-h1z1-mode/

Greene states:
- Battle Royale began inside the DayZ mod ecosystem;
- he moved the project to Arma 3 after its alpha period because it was a better engine for his purposes and because zombie/random-death behaviour damaged fairness;
- the Arma 3 mod had exceeded 200,000 downloads and around 50,000 monthly active players by his stated count;
- about 3,000 squads had signed up for planned squad competition;
- an Extra Life event drew roughly 55,000 Twitch viewers by his account;
- he postponed a serious competitive league because Arma 3 multiplayer desync/lag made prize competition unfair;
- SOE licensed Battle Royale non-exclusively and brought him in as a consultant for H1Z1;
- his eventual ambition was still a standalone Battle Royale, but at that moment he valued bringing the mode into multiple games and growing the community.

Supports:
- substantial public validation existed before PUBG;
- Greene was already using technical constraints and fairness criteria to decide what *not* to commercialize competitively;
- H1Z1 was not the origin of his rule set;
- commercial adoption followed a visible mod/community track record.

Boundary:
- download/MAU/squad/viewer figures are creator-reported, not independently audited financial or platform data.

## E003 — PC Gamer 2017: Bluehole invitation and commercial amplification

- Class: P1/S1 — reported feature with direct Greene quotations near launch
- Source: Anthony McGlynn, PC Gamer, `From modder to pro, the making of PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds`, 2017-06-22
- URL: https://www.pcgamer.com/from-modder-to-pro-the-making-of-playerunknowns-battleground/

Source-derived facts / testimony:
- Bluehole executive Chang-Han Kim contacted Greene directly;
- Greene flew to South Korea, found Bluehole's standalone Battle Royale proposal closely aligned with his own vision, and joined development soon after;
- PUBG development began in early 2016 using Unreal Engine 4;
- the commercial development team was reported at roughly three dozen people during the interview period;
- Arma 3 and H1Z1 Battle Royale already had significant streamer visibility before PUBG;
- Greene describes seeing a streamer playing the Arma 3 mod to around 15,000 viewers as a moment when he better understood the spectator/market potential of the format.

Supports:
- `community validation → company invitation → corporate production scale`;
- PUBG should not be described as a solo product;
- streamer visibility functioned before final product launch as both market access and behavioural evidence.

Boundary:
- one near-launch profile does not settle all internal budget, authority, contributor or Bluehole strategy questions.

## E004 — GamesBeat 2019: server files, Survivor GameZ frustration, and artifact-first learning

- Class: P1 — direct creator conversation
- Source: GamesBeat, `How Brendan Greene created the PUBG phenomenon — in conversation with Rami Ismail`, 2019
- URL: https://gamesbeat.com/brendan-greene-and-rami-ismail/

Useful creator testimony:
- Greene describes opening Arma II / DayZ server files and discovering that he could understand enough of the scripts to change behaviour;
- he wanted to participate in Survivor GameZ-style play but, as a non-streamer, could not simply enter the existing spectacle;
- instead of waiting for formal permission, he used the moddable game as a production substrate for the experience he wanted;
- he did not enter through conventional game-development credentials.

Supports:
- `artifact-first credential`;
- modding as a low-cost problem-definition and learning environment;
- a personal unmet desire can become a production question before it becomes a market thesis.

Boundary:
- direct retrospective storytelling can compress chronology; exact first-build dates still require contemporaneous archive recovery.

## E007 — PCGamesN 2017: rule intent between Arma and H1Z1

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: PCGamesN, `Getting to know PlayerUnknown`, 2017
- URL: https://www.pcgamesn.com/playerunknowns-battlegrounds/pubg-battlegrounds-brendan-greene

Greene states:
- the original mod began because he wanted to play an experience like Survivor GameZ that ordinary players could access;
- he had already scripted his own DayZ server before creating the mod;
- for PUBG he wanted a middle ground between Arma 3's simulation depth and H1Z1's accessibility.

Supports:
- product definition came from a specific experiential gap, not simply from maximizing feature count;
- PUBG's commercial design involved selective subtraction/translation of two earlier implementations.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED

- Greene entered commercial game development through modding rather than a conventional studio career ladder;
- the modern PLAYERUNKNOWN Battle Royale lineage predates PUBG and was publicly playable in DayZ/Arma;
- the Arma 3 mod had substantial community adoption and streamer/event visibility before the Bluehole project;
- H1Z1 represents a commercial licensing/consulting transition, not the origin of the mode;
- Bluehole recruited Greene after the rule set/community already existed;
- PUBG was produced by a commercial company/team and must not be described as a solo indie game;
- Greene's role changed from direct mod maker to creative director/manager.

### SUPPORTED / NEEDS MORE GRANULARITY

- modding functioned as an alternative credential system: public artifacts and community behaviour made Greene legible to SOE/Bluehole before traditional credentials;
- streamers operated not only as marketing but as an early observable signal of repeatability/spectator appeal;
- the lineage demonstrates how a high-value rule innovation can be independently searched and later corporately amplified.

### PARTIAL / NEXT VERIFY

- exact chronology of DayZCherno+ / first Battle Royale builds and servers;
- collaborator and server-admin contribution boundaries in 2013–2014;
- exact design lineage of shrinking zone, plane insertion, loot randomization and other rules;
- SOE licensing/consulting contract terms and compensation;
- Bluehole initial budget, hiring sequence and team headcount over time;
- PUBG Early Access growth decomposition across streamers, Steam discovery, Korean/Chinese/global audiences;
- Greene's Brazil/Ireland runway and any welfare/unemployment period under strongest primary documentation;
- management/production failures during the transition from mod creator to director.
