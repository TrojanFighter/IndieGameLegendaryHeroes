## E001 — Cindering: DevEx testimony

- Class: P1 — developer retrospective self-report
- Source: Roblox Developer Forum, Cindering / Brian Wilson
- Publication date: 2019-05-21
- URL: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/issues-with-the-developer-exchange-testimonies-from-the-roblox-dev-community/282656/9

Source-derived facts:
- Cindering says he joined Roblox in 2009 and initially made small games as a hobby;
- he did not take Roblox seriously as a career until DevEx appeared in 2013 and later expanded;
- he and two friends released the original Roblox High School in 2014;
- he entered college in 2016 to study computer science and retained college as a Plan B;
- after DevEx improved and the platform grew, he increasingly regarded Roblox as viable;
- for Roblox High School 2 in 2018 he scripted full-time in the accelerator program while teammates worked remotely part-time on building/modeling and other tasks;
- after the game gained traction he dropped college classes and moved into full-time Roblox development;
- he explicitly described income volatility, the need for a safety net, and difficulty funding a whole full-time team at then-current DevEx economics.

Supports:
- C003: visible small-team success followed years of platform-specific capability accumulation;
- C006: this career path did not require prior AAA employment;
- C008: platform infrastructure and monetization reduced some entry barriers while platform economics remained a constraint;
- C011: the representative-game development window undercounts years of hobby/professionalizing work.

Boundary:
- this is a successful creator's retrospective account, not a representative sample;
- it does not disclose exact earnings, household support or the causal contribution of each DevEx rate change.

## E002 — Cinder Studio full-time scripter hiring

- Class: P0 — contemporaneous studio job post
- Source: Roblox Developer Forum, Cindering / Cinder Studio
- Publication date: 2021-06-04
- URL: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/closed-hiring-full-time-scripter-for-roblox-high-school-2/1272823

Source-derived facts:
- Cindering identified himself as Brian and described Roblox High School 2 as having 400M+ visits since its December 2018 launch;
- the post listed a studio team and sought a long-term full-time scripter for 30–40 hours per week;
- compensation was stated as 2,000,000 Robux / $7,000 USD per month / $84,000 annual equivalent via group funds;
- the role required a contract and was framed as continuing beyond a single update.

Supports:
- platform-native creator income can mature into real labor demand and studio organization;
- contributor audit: Cinder Studio should not be described as a literal solo operation after success;
- C008: the platform functions not only as a publishing surface but also as a labor market and payment rail.

Boundary:
- advertised compensation does not prove how long the role lasted or total studio payroll;
- 2021 team structure cannot be projected backward onto the 2014 original Roblox High School.

## E003 — Alex Binello / MeepCity direct interview

- Class: S1 — edited business press with direct developer interview
- Source: Business Insider article syndicated by Yahoo Finance
- Author: Matt Weinberger
- Publication date: 2018-07-22
- URL: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/self-taught-23-old-programmer-202906042.html

Source-derived facts:
- Binello described himself as self-taught and had been building on Roblox from a young age;
- when Roblox lacked a revenue-sharing path circa 2012, he tried smartphone games;
- he said Pears to Pairs reached about 50,000 downloads but failed to become a real business;
- he worked part-time at GameStop while pursuing independent game development;
- after Roblox introduced revenue sharing / DevEx in 2013, he returned because his platform familiarity gave him an advantage;
- Roblox invited him to a Silicon Valley internship in summer 2015; he said that income let him quit GameStop;
- MeepCity later became his breakout product.

Supports:
- C003: breakout success followed prior platform experience and an unsuccessful off-platform attempt;
- C006: conventional AAA employment was not necessary in this path;
- C008: DevEx and platform familiarity changed the opportunity set;
- C011: MeepCity's visible development period omits years of prior production and employment runway.

Boundary:
- reported download numbers and career history rely substantially on Binello's own recollection;
- article success metrics should not be treated as audited financial statements.

## E004 — asimo3089: `18 Years of Roblox` retrospective

- Class: P1 — developer retrospective self-report
- Source: Roblox Developer Forum, asimo3089
- URL: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/18-years-of-roblox/61462/33

Source-derived facts:
- asimo3089 says a large part of 2015 went to Port Gloom, his largest project to that point;
- the rest of 2015 included contract work under Roblox connected to a mobile launch;
- in 2016 he continued work under Roblox related to Xbox and teamed with badcc on Volt;
- in 2017 he and badcc developed Jailbreak after playing Prison Life and wanting more from the concept;
- he learned Blender for vehicle bodies while badcc developed cars;
- he says Jailbreak released on April 21 after substantial Twitter hype, followed by weekly updates during the summer and monthly updates later.

Supports:
- C003 / C011: Jailbreak's short visible production window sat on top of prior projects, paid platform work and an established collaboration;
- C006: platform-native professionalization can substitute for conventional studio employment;
- C010: launch had explicit pre-release market activity, not zero marketing.

Boundary:
- the post is retrospective and does not quantify contract compensation or the scale of the Twitter audience;
- it cannot by itself establish the full contributor list for Jailbreak.

## E005 — badcc contemporaneous Jailbreak launch thread

- Class: P0 — contemporaneous developer post
- Source: Roblox Developer Forum, badcc / Alex Balfanz
- Publication date: 2017-04-22
- URL: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/what-are-you-working-on-currently-2017/32228?page=110

Source-derived facts:
- badcc reported that he and asimo3089 had just released Jailbreak;
- he first reported a 40,000 concurrent-player peak, then edited with 60,000 and 75,000 as traffic rose;
- he said the volume of feedback and analytics traffic exceeded expectations and his web server failed;
- he planned to add Google Cloud capacity to handle the load.

Supports:
- launch scale was a genuine right-tail event even for the developers;
- successful platform launches can abruptly create infrastructure and live-operations work;
- C010: immediate player feedback and platform distribution are part of the product/market system.

Boundary:
- first-day CCU does not establish causation for why the game became successful;
- the post does not identify all sources of traffic or platform promotion.

## E006 — GamesBeat 2017: Roblox's young developers / Alex Balfanz

- Class: S1 — professional industry press with direct interviews
- Source: GamesBeat, Dean Takahashi
- Publication date: 2017-07-21
- URL: https://gamesbeat.com/the-deanbeat-robloxs-kid-developers-make-enough-robucks-to-pay-for-college/

Source-derived facts:
- GamesBeat reported Balfanz was 18 and had been using Roblox for roughly nine years;
- he began the specific Jailbreak project in January 2017 and released it after about four months;
- three months after launch it had been played about 210 million times;
- Balfanz said Roblox had been easy to enter and could support a full career;
- the article reported he had generated enough money to pay for college.

Supports:
- C003 / C011: the famous “four-month” figure describes the product's concentrated development window, not the creator's capability-formation window;
- C006: the route did not depend on AAA employment;
- platform monetization could materially change education/career choices for a top creator.

Boundary:
- the college-funding statement is a press report, not an audited earnings figure;
- the piece focuses on exceptional successful creators and carries strong survivor-selection bias.

## E007 — Roblox Creator Hub monetization / current creator distribution

- Class: P0 — official platform documentation
- Source: Roblox Creator Hub
- Access date: 2026-10-04
- URL: https://create.roblox.com/docs/monetize

Official facts:
- Roblox describes instant publishing across multiple device platforms and supplies hosting/support, user acquisition, translation and compliance infrastructure;
- Roblox says its top 1,000 creators averaged $1.3M in 2025;
- it also says more than 35,000 creators were in DevEx and the median DevEx creator received $1,550 during the twelve months ended December 31, 2025.

Supports:
- C008: platform infrastructure can absorb functions a standalone studio would otherwise need to source;
- survivor-bias boundary: earnings are extremely skewed, so headliner creators cannot stand in for the typical creator;
- C010: platform discovery and user acquisition are market infrastructure, not an absence of marketing.

Boundary:
- these are 2025–2026 platform conditions and cannot be retroactively used to quantify 2013–2018 odds or economics;
- official platform materials have promotional incentives and should be paired with filings / independent evidence.

## E008 — Roblox 2025 Form 10-K / DevEx governance

- Class: P0 — regulatory filing
- Source: Roblox Corporation, Form 10-K for year ended 2025-12-31
- URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm

Official facts:
- as of 2025-12-31, more than 35,500 creators were registered and qualified for DevEx;
- more than 23,500 creators received fiat payments through DevEx during 2025;
- qualification depends on platform-defined requirements and eligible Earned Robux;
- current Creator Hub terms likewise reserve Roblox discretion over what qualifies as Earned Robux and the applicable exchange rate.

Supports:
- C008: creator businesses gain integrated monetization infrastructure but remain dependent on platform governance;
- provides a denominator/boundary against treating a handful of famous creators as representative.

Boundary:
- current DevEx scale is not evidence of the historical 2013–2018 creator population or success probability;
- registration/payment counts alone do not reveal the full income distribution.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- the visible Roblox hits in this cluster were preceded by years of platform-native production and learning;
- DevEx and paid Roblox opportunities could convert hobby skill into career runway;
- platform infrastructure collapses several functions normally separated across engine, publisher, storefront, server provider, payment rail and labor market;
- the “few months to make the hit” narrative materially undercounts capability formation;
- market access existed through platform discovery, existing community and explicit social promotion.

### SUPPORTED WITH BOUNDARY
- Roblox can operate as a vertically integrated apprenticeship environment for some creators;
- platform-native careers can grow into real studio employment structures;
- lower friction from learning to publishing likely increases experimentation opportunity, but the size of that causal effect is not yet measured.

### UNKNOWN / NEEDS COUNTERFACTUAL
- success rate for ordinary creators following a similar path;
- how much homepage/discovery placement versus product quality/network effects explains each breakout;
- complete early financial runway and family support for each creator;
- full contributor boundary for Jailbreak;
- how well platform-native capability transfers to fully independent PC/console production.
