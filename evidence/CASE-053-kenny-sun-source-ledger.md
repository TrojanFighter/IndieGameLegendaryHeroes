## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- Kenny made and published small games before his commercial career and used Newgrounds / Kongregate as early output surfaces.
- Circa Infinity grew from a Ludum Dare prototype into a commercial game through an extended student / incubator period.
- Circa Infinity helped Kenny enter Harmonix as a programmer, by his own retrospective account.
- he continued personal games on weekends while at Harmonix, and their income eventually became sufficient for him to leave in 2018.
- Hatbox was first shelved because its scope exceeded the free time available under full-time employment, then restarted after his production situation changed.
- Raw Fury was deliberately used to add funding and release capabilities Kenny did not want to internalize.
- BALL x PIT is Kenny’s first explicitly team-led project and used a named specialist core plus Devolver publishing support.
- BALL x PIT exceeded one million Steam units in its first ten weeks.

### SUPPORTED WITH BOUNDARY
- Kenny is a strong longitudinal capability-accretion case: successive projects, employment and external peripheries visibly changed his feasible production frontier.
- his production path can be read as capability-shaped solo work gradually transitioning into capability-expanded author-led production.
- concert photography is part of his creative prehistory, but its direct causal effect on game visuals remains unproven.

### UNKNOWN / DO NOT INFER
- NYU double major in Computer Science and Mathematics;
- Cisco employment immediately after graduation;
- Cisco chosen primarily to satisfy parents / family expectations;
- precise New York / New Jersey childhood geography;
- concert photography directly caused later screen-shake / visual-rhythm design;
- Harmonix directly taught the later “feel” grammar;
- Raw Fury / Devolver advance, revenue-share, recoup, IP, milestone, approval or ownership terms;
- BALL x PIT collaborator employment / equity / decision-rights structure.
