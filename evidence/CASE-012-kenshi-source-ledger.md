## E001 — Chris Hunt interview on eight-year development

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: Siliconera, `Kenshi's Eight Year Development Journey From One-Man RPG To A Team's Success`, Chris Priestman, 2015
- URL: https://www.siliconera.com/kenshis-eight-year-development-journey-from-one-man-rpg-to-a-teams-success/
- Verified: 2026-10-09, 原文逐字核对

Chris Hunt states:
- he had started Kenshi around eight years earlier;
- for the first five or six years he worked alone on it full-time while juggling a minimum-wage night security job to survive;
- much of that time went into fundamental systems and reaching a playable state;
- **before Greenlight in 2013 he was alpha funding the game himself through his own website, and that income was enough to support himself and hire freelancers**;
- Steam Early Access gave him the funding he needed to get a team together;
- over roughly two years he built a small team including programming, world design, PR/writing and art roles.

Direct quote (verbatim):

> "I wouldn't be able to fund Kenshi's development without Early Access. Before we got Greenlit in 2013, I was alpha funding it myself through my own website, which was enough to support myself and hire freelancers. Steam Early Access, however, has given me the funding I need to get a team together and make progress."

Supports:
- C002: sustainable recurring income can be more important than a single financing event.
- C003/C011: the later studio form rests on years of solo foundation.
- C010: market access is part of production, and it can begin before a platform storefront.

Correction note (2026-10-09):
- This entry previously stopped at "after Steam Early Access he could afford additional manpower". That summary dropped the pre-Steam own-site alpha phase, and the chapter/profile prose built on it turned Steam Early Access into the first revenue that funded production. The original interview contradicts that sequence.

## E002 — Lo-Fi Games official history / fact sheet

- Class: P1 — official institutional retrospective
- Source: Lo-Fi Games, `Fact Sheet`
- URL: https://lofigames.com/press/

Official history states:
- Hunt spent his early twenties working as a game programmer;
- in 2008 he left and worked on Kenshi full-time while doing night security shifts to scrape by;
- after Steam Greenlight / Early Access in 2013, Kenshi raised enough money to fund a small team.

Supports:
- professional programming prehistory;
- salary cross-subsidy → own-site paid alpha → platform Early Access → team expansion sequence.

Boundary:
- does not give exact wages, savings, EA revenue or total budget.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- long solo phase existed but did not cover the whole production history.

- Steam Early Access income enabled team expansion beyond that.
