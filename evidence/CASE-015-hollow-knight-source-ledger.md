## E002 — Nintendo Australia direct Team Cherry interview

- Class: P1 — direct creator retrospective
- Source: Nintendo Australia, `The Metamorphosis of Hollow Knight, with Team Cherry`, 2021-07-20
- URL: https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/the-metamorphosis-of-hollow-knight-with-team-cherry-aussie-developer-interview/

Team Cherry states:
- studio is a three-man team at interview time;
- Hollow Knight started as a game-jam project;
- the jam's severe time constraint forced a small, focused concept that later grew.

Direct quotes (verbatim, page re-read 2026-10-09):

The three members, named (the earlier summary left the third unnamed):
- "We're a three-man studio in Adelaide, South Australia – Ari Gibson, William Pellen and Jack Vine. The team was formed around a mutual love of classic adventure games like Zelda II: The Adventure of Link and Faxanadu…"

The jam, including its theme:
- "Hollow Knight actually started as a game jam project!"
- "During a game jam, the idea is to build a small but fully-formed game within a tight time limit (usually about 48 hours) with a small team and focused on a central theme. The theme for the jam was 'Beneath the Surface'…"

On the location, in the studio's own words:
- "It's good! Adelaide is a cosy place, super affordable, and has lots of great cafés and restaurants tucked away…"

Supports:
- C011: commercial project had a jam/prototype prehistory.
- C007: tiny constrained prototypes can seed much larger later scope.

Additional boundary:
- Naming Jack Vine closes a gap in the contributor audit; it does not establish his task split across the whole project.
- The Adelaide line is platform-interview framing and should be read alongside the cost-of-living statement in E003, not instead of it.

## E003 — Source Gaming interview conducted 2018

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview, publication delayed to 2025
- Source: Source Gaming, `Straight from the Source: Ari Gibson & William Pellen (Team Cherry)`
- URL: https://sourcegaming.info/2025/04/09/straight-from-the-source-team-cherry/

Directly stated production conditions:
- Gibson had savings from a successful animation business;
- Pellen's partner worked full time;
- the development team's third member also had a partner working full time;
- Adelaide's low cost of living helped the crowdfunding budget last;
- Indie Fund provided additional late-stage financing for extra desired content.

Direct quotes (verbatim, body re-read 2026-10-09; interview conducted at BitSummit 2018, published 2025):

On why a modest budget stretched as far as it did:
- "the simple answer is, yes, that budget did sustain us but it was because of our specific situation. We don't have children and William has a partner that works full time. I had an animation business before that [Mechanical Apple] was quite successful, so I had some [savings]…"
- "Then probably the other part of all this that helped us was living in Adelaide, where the cost of living is very cheap. So there were a number of factors that worked for us and allowed us to make a game like Hollow Knight on quite a low budget."

On the target figure and how it was set:
- "the goal that we set was something like AU$35,000."
- "we were debating it before the kickstarter. I think we changed it up from like AU$30,000 a few days before it went live."
- "But that number was to create a Metroidvania that was very small. It was really like a small thing."
- "And then the success of the campaign allowed us to make it a level bigger."

On Indie Fund, and what it was for:
- "there are all these extra things we really want to get in there but they aren't essential for us to release the game. But they were things we thought would make the game really great and for that, we actually approached Indie Fund."

Localisation, including fan labour the ledger did not previously record:
- "we paid for some translations and then fans ended up coming to us and saying 'this is awful. We will help you fix it.' And they did."
- "a lot of those fans have now, because we've been releasing additional content packs, just become the translators for their respective [languages]."

A design constraint stated as a deliberate choice:
- "That was a deliberate design choice from the very start. Hollow Knight is influenced by NES-era games and it's intended to have the qualities of NES-era games…"
- "when we started development one of the decisions we made was that kind of really straight terrain."

Supports:
- C001/C002/C012: headline funding cannot be interpreted without household and location context.

Additional boundary:
- "We don't have children" is a household condition the earlier summary omitted.
- The fan-translation work is a contributor-audit item: it extends the production perimeter past the three-person core and past paid contractors, and it was initially a response to paid translations the players judged poor.
- These are 2018 recollections of 2014–2015 conditions.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- jam prototypes preceded the commercial project;
- Kickstarter was one component of a broader runway structure;
- household income, prior savings and local cost of living mattered;
- external contributors existed beyond the core team;
- tool migration changed production economics.

### UNKNOWN
- complete budget and burn rate;
- exact value/terms of Indie Fund financing;
- full contributor/outsourcing map;
- counterfactual: whether the same scope would have survived in a higher-cost city.
