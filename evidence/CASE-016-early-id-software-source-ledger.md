## E001 — John Romero on small-scale development

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: How To Market A Game, `John Romero on his book Doom Guy and developing games at a small scale`, 2023-09-25
- URL: https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/09/25/john-romero-on-his-book-doom-guy-and-developing-games-at-a-small-scale/

Romero states:
- Gamer's Edge required frequent shipped games;
- he and Carmack also made games outside normal work hours;
- the team estimated how many levels/data they could finish inside fixed deadlines;
- they deliberately did not scope beyond the finite time they knew they had.

Supports:
- C003/C011: repeated shipping preceded id's famous releases.
- C007: scope was derived from constraints rather than copied from larger games.

## E002 — Tom Hall interview on moonlighting

- Class: P1 — direct creator retrospective
- Source: Episodic Content Magazine, `The Science of Happiness: An Interview with Tom Hall – Part 2`, 2015
- URL: https://episodiccontentmag.com/2015/04/17/tomhall2/

Hall states:
- Commander Keen was developed by moonlighting at night/weekends for roughly two and a half months;
- the team remained under Softdisk obligations and agreed to continue making games as part of avoiding litigation;
- Hall stayed temporarily to train a replacement while also working for id.

Supports:
- C005: paid employment / obligations and original development overlapped.
- C002: full-time independence followed, rather than preceded, proof of revenue.

## E003 — Scott Miller interview on shareware and id transition

- Class: P1 — direct publisher/founder retrospective
- Source: Episodic Content Magazine, `Bigger in Texas: An Interview with Scott Miller, Part 2`, 2015
- URL: https://episodiccontentmag.com/2015/08/17/bigger-in-texas-an-interview-with-scott-miller-part-2/

Miller states:
- id developed Keen while still at Softdisk, including use of Softdisk computers;
- Keen's shareware success convinced the team that they could leave Softdisk and do this full time;
- legal/contractual obligations complicated the transition.

Supports:
- distribution/revenue was part of organizational formation.

## E007 — January 1994 *Game Developer*: production and business snapshot immediately after DOOM

- Class: P0/S1 — contemporaneous trade-press feature with direct John Carmack / id quotations
- Source: Alexander Antoniades, `Monsters From the Id: The Making of Doom`, originally *Game Developer* premiere issue, January 1994; reprinted 2009
- URL: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/the-game-developer-archives-monsters-from-the-id-the-making-of-i-doom-i-

Contemporaneous facts / observations include:
- id was a seven-person company at the time of reporting;
- Wolfenstein 3D used shareware distribution and had broken id's earlier Commander Keen shareware record;
- the article compares shareware direct economics with retail distribution and describes the unusually high retained margin of the former;
- id's main development environment included NeXTStep; Romero's DoomEd took about five human-months to build;
- Carmack states that DOOM was primarily written in ANSI C with only a small number of assembler routines, in contrast to assumptions that id relied mainly on assembly;
- the development environment and editor were explicitly used to reduce iteration/porting friction;
- Tom Hall's departure is described as a consequence of conflict over the design direction of DOOM;
- id decided to take over DOOM distribution rather than continue routing orders through Apogee, while outsourcing phone-order handling to Digital Magnetics;
- id planned to expose technical specifications and tolerated/encouraged player modification rather than treating all modification as hostile;
- the article documents external contributors/contractors for graphics/models, sound/network support and distribution operations.

Supports:
- C002/C010: market/distribution architecture is part of production optionality;
- C004/C007: toolchain and code-language choices follow team bottlenecks rather than ideology;
- C011: rapid prior releases and accumulated technology precede DOOM;
- contributor-boundary audit: DOOM was a small-core production, not a two-person or fully internal product.

Boundary:
- sales/profit figures are valuable because they are contemporaneous, but still reported press figures rather than audited company financials.

## E008 — WIRED 1996 on id's open/modding ecology

- Class: P0/S1 — contemporaneous reported profile
- Source: WIRED, `The Egos at Id`, 1996
- URL: https://www.wired.com/1996/08/id/

Source-derived observations:
- id's philosophy of sharing code/specifications and tolerating modification became part of the DOOM phenomenon;
- publication of specifications helped support a large ecosystem of maps, editors and add-on content;
- user creativity extended the life and reach of the product even where id did not directly capture all downstream revenue.

Supports:
- C010: distribution/community architecture can amplify market access and product longevity;
- external community can function as a production/perpetuation layer around a small core team.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- Commander Keen was built on top of intensive professional shipping experience;
- original development overlapped paid employment and contractual obligations;
- shareware revenue helped purchase organizational independence;
- finite production time directly shaped scope;
- by the Wolfenstein/DOOM period, id deliberately invested in internal tools and a development environment that reduced iteration/porting friction;
- DOOM's business boundary changed from publisher/distributor dependence toward more direct strategic control plus outsourced operations;
- the team explicitly tolerated/encouraged modification and technical openness, helping create an external content/tool ecosystem;
- DOOM remained a small-core project with meaningful specialist/external contribution boundaries.

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED AS A RESEARCH METHOD
- *Masters of Doom* is high-value as a narrative and hypothesis-generating source, but not sufficient as sole evidence for disputed factual claims;
- *DOOM Guy*, contemporary Game Developer/WIRED coverage and other participant accounts should be used to triangulate it.

### PARTIAL / NEXT VERIFY
- exact first Apogee advance/royalty amounts under strongest primary documentation;
- complete economic value of Softdisk hardware/wages;
- audited Wolfenstein/DOOM unit economics and overseas split;
- cost/contract details for Digital Magnetics and other external contributors;
- causal contribution of WAD/modding openness to sales, longevity, recruitment and brand growth;
- point-by-point conflict map between Kushner, Romero, Carmack, Hall and other participant accounts.
