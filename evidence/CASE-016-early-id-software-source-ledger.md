## E001 — John Romero on small-scale development

- Class: P1 — direct creator interview
- Source: How To Market A Game, `John Romero on his book Doom Guy and developing games at a small scale`, 2023-09-25
- URL: https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/09/25/john-romero-on-his-book-doom-guy-and-developing-games-at-a-small-scale/

Romero states:
- Gamer's Edge required frequent shipped games;
- he and Carmack also made games outside normal work hours;
- the team estimated how many levels/data they could finish inside fixed deadlines;
- they deliberately did not scope beyond the finite time they knew they had.

Supports:
- C003/C011: repeated shipping preceded id's famous releases.
- C007: scope was derived from constraints rather than copied from larger games.

## E002 — Tom Hall interview on moonlighting

- Class: P1 — direct creator retrospective
- Source: Episodic Content Magazine, `The Science of Happiness: An Interview with Tom Hall – Part 2`, 2015
- URL: https://episodiccontentmag.com/2015/04/17/tomhall2/

Hall states:
- Commander Keen was developed by moonlighting at night/weekends for roughly two and a half months;
- the team remained under Softdisk obligations and agreed to continue making games as part of avoiding litigation;
- Hall stayed temporarily to train a replacement while also working for id.

Supports:
- C005: paid employment / obligations and original development overlapped.
- C002: full-time independence followed, rather than preceded, proof of revenue.

## E003 — Scott Miller interview on shareware and id transition

- Class: P1 — direct publisher/founder retrospective
- Source: Episodic Content Magazine, `Bigger in Texas: An Interview with Scott Miller, Part 2`, 2015
- URL: https://episodiccontentmag.com/2015/08/17/bigger-in-texas-an-interview-with-scott-miller-part-2/

Miller states:
- id developed Keen while still at Softdisk, including use of Softdisk computers;
- Keen's shareware success convinced the team that they could leave Softdisk and do this full time;
- legal/contractual obligations complicated the transition.

Supports:
- distribution/revenue was part of organizational formation.
