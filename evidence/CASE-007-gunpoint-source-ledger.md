## E001 — Gunpoint Development Breakdown

- Class: P1 — creator postmortem
- Source: Tom Francis, `Gunpoint Development Breakdown`, 2013-10-15
- URL: https://www.pentadact.com/2013-10-15-gunpoint-development-breakdown/

Direct creator account:
- Francis blogged development from the first weekend through the game's roughly three-year development.
- He recruited artists by open sample submissions; selected John Roberts and Fabian van Dommelen.
- Music contributors included Ryan Ike, John Robert Matz and Francisco Cerda.
- The remote team was distributed internationally and communicated primarily by email.
- Once the project became commercial, contributors agreed to revenue shares based on contribution.
- Francis says the second explanatory video drove widespread press coverage, first ~7,000 Twitter followers, ~15,000 testers and strong demand that the project be sold rather than released free.
- Valve contacted him about Steam distribution before Greenlight.

Supports:
- C003: core solo ownership != no contributors.
- C010: devlog/video/press/platform access were active market infrastructure.
- C004/C007: low-cost production used a deliberately different organization and representation model.

Boundary:
- Creator postmortem is retrospective and naturally selects what Francis considered important.
- Tester/follower numbers are acquisition/community signals, not sales or retention.

## E004 — Road to the IGF: journalism as taste / selection capital

- Class: P1/S1 — direct creator interview in professional trade media
- Source: Game Developer / Gamasutra, `Road to the IGF: Tom Francis' Gunpoint`, 2012
- URL: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-tom-francis-i-gunpoint-i-

Direct creator account:
- Francis says he had no prior game-development background and began making a game partly because, as a critic, he kept forming ideas about how games could be improved and wanted to test whether those ideas were actually right.
- He says discovering that *Spelunky* was made in GameMaker was a practical trigger: the tool seemed novice-friendly, and within less than a month he had a movement prototype that could be sent to testers.
- Asked whether games journalism helped, he says it definitely did. The most useful part was having an overview of what was already out there and what was notable among it.
- He explicitly connects that overview to judging whether an idea is unusual enough to be interesting: he did not know whether Gunpoint's central mechanic would work, but believed that if it worked it would at least be unusual.
- He then describes a reinforcing chain: unusual idea → public interest → ability to recruit artists → better presentation → more interest → ability to recruit musicians.

Supports:
- C003/C011: the nominally first commercial game sat on years of analysis/taste accumulation.
- C004: creator judgment can manufacture production conditions by selecting ideas that are both distinctive and testable.
- C010: product distinctiveness and public attention were already coupled to contributor acquisition before launch.

Boundary:
- “Taste” is an analytical label used by this project; Francis describes specific habits and advantages rather than claiming a general theory that taste guarantees success.
- His PC Gamer role also created network/visibility advantages; the evidence does not isolate taste from occupational access.

## E006 — Non-Stick Plan: compress the thing you love into a small rule system

- Class: P1 — creator design-method post
- Source: Tom Francis, `Game Design: The Non-Stick Plan`, 2014-01-25
- URL: https://www.pentadact.com/2014-01-25-game-design-the-non-stick-plan/

Direct creator account:
- Francis says his projects often start from excitement about something in another game, film or imagined design.
- The transition from “I like this” to “I want to make a game about this” happens when he becomes convinced that the exciting quality can be compressed into a simple set of rules that regenerate the excitement repeatedly.
- For Gunpoint, the starting excitement included finding clever ways to infiltrate buildings in *Deus Ex*.
- He describes resolving broad design unknowns by finding reusable classifications/templates rather than hand-authoring every case; for Gunpoint, hackable objects became electrical devices that could trigger whatever they were connected to.

Supports:
- C007: small-team design advantage can come from changing representation and system structure rather than reproducing source-game scope.
- C004: taste becomes production-relevant when it identifies the emotional/interactive essence worth preserving and discards the expensive shell around it.

Boundary:
- Written after Gunpoint's release and therefore partly retrospective/formalized method.
- Does not prove every Gunpoint decision followed this explicit framework at the time it was made.

## E007 — Longitudinal check: criticism → explicit difference → new game

- Class: P1/S1 — direct creator interview, later project
- Source: PC Gamer, `XCOM 2's problems inspired a whole other tactics game`, 2020-11-10
- URL: https://www.pcgamer.com/tactical-breach-wizards-interview/

Direct creator account:
- Francis describes his process as playing a game, articulating what he wishes were different, then making a game that does it that way.
- For *Tactical Breach Wizards*, he began from both affection for and detailed objections to *XCOM 2*, then converted those objections into concrete design constraints such as simpler combat spaces and free rewind.

Supports:
- The Gunpoint-era critic→designer pattern persisted beyond one lucky first project.
- “Taste” in this case is not merely preference; it is repeated comparative diagnosis translated into executable design constraints.

Boundary:
- This is evidence about Francis' later stable method, not direct evidence that every Gunpoint feature originated the same way.

## E008 — Scope taste: cutting authored spectacle that added little as a game

- Class: P0 — contemporaneous creator development post
- Source: Tom Francis, `Gunpoint And The Other Game`, 2010-10-25
- URL: https://pentadact.wordpress.com/2010/10/25/gunpoint-and-the-other-game/
- Access date: 2026-10-09
- Classification correction: the dated post records an ongoing project decision, rather than a later recollection. This corrects the previous P1 label, without upgrading its causal reach.

Contemporaneous creator account:
- Francis describes realizing that he had automatically included scripted sequences, major characters and predetermined developments because he had already imagined and written them.
- Re-examining the roadmap, he noticed how much coding those non-interactive sequences required relative to how little they added “as a game.”
- He therefore treats them as a scope/design mistake rather than preserving them because work or writing had already been invested.

Supports:
- C007: scope control can be driven by a clear hierarchy of what the creator believes the game is *for*.
- C004: taste has a negative function as well as a positive one — it tells the creator what not to build.

Boundary:
- This is one concrete decision, not proof that Francis always avoided sunk-cost or scope errors.

## E015 — Tactical Breach Wizards release endpoint

- Case ID: CASE-007
- Source class: P0
- Source title: Tactical Breach Wizards Is Out!
- Author / organization: Tom Francis / personal blog
- Publication date: 2024-08-22
- URL: https://www.pentadact.com/2024-08-22-tactical-breach-wizards-is-out/
- Access date: 2026-10-09
- Verification Status: PARTIAL (creator release announcement read)

The announcement states that the team worked on the game for 6.8 years. This is an elapsed project duration, not 6.8 years of one person's full-time hours. It supports the release endpoint, not lifetime sales or a complete contributor perimeter. E007 remains the authority for the 2020 design interview.

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
- Gunpoint was not literally a one-human production.
- Development was publicly documented over about three years.
- Salary employment / sabbatical protected much of the pre-launch development period.
- Market access included devlogs, videos, testers, media, IGF and Steam.
- Francis developed Gunpoint alongside a professional game-criticism career. The 2013 talk's nine-year framing includes the development period; it is not nine years completed before starting.
- He explicitly identifies broad market/game literacy as useful for judging whether an idea is unusual enough to attract interest.
- His documented design method repeatedly turns likes/dislikes into explicit, testable design constraints and favors compressing an experience into reusable rules.

### SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION
- For this case, **taste is a form of capability capital**: comparative exposure + articulated preference + willingness to test + the discipline to cut things that do not serve the chosen experience.
- This helps explain why “little programming experience” did not mean “little relevant prehistory.”

### FORBIDDEN INFERENCE
- Good taste by itself produces successful games.
- Being a critic/journalist is generally a superior route into game development.
- Francis' media employment can be reduced to taste alone; it also supplied salary, network, visibility and professional communication skill.
- An unusual idea is sufficient for market success.

### UNKNOWN
- Full opportunity cost.
- Contributor hours and final revenue shares.
- Exact causal advantage from Francis' journalist identity versus product quality, public development and occupational network.
- How much of Gunpoint's final commercial performance can be attributed specifically to idea selection versus execution, distribution and luck.
