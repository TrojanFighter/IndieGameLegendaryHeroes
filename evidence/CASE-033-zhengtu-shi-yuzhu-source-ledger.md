## E001 — Giant Interactive 2007 F-1: business model, market structure, distribution and ZT economics

- Source class: P0 — SEC registration filing / company financial disclosure
- Title: Form F-1 Registration Statement, Giant Interactive Group Inc.
- Author / Institution: Giant Interactive Group Inc. / U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission
- Published: 2007-10-12
- Accessed: 2026-10-05
- URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1415016/000119312507217827/df1.htm
- Claim use:
  - ZT Online was commercially launched in January 2006 as a free-to-play MMO;
  - the filing says Chinese F2P MMOs had been introduced by 2003 and by 2006 accounted for about 51.6% of online-game revenue versus 47.9% for pay-to-play, so ZT did not invent F2P;
  - 2006 online-game net revenue was RMB408.5m and 2007 H1 online-game net revenue RMB683.8m; the filing says these periods' online-game revenue came from ZT Online;
  - the company describes more than 140 product-development software/platform/media staff and weekly/quarterly updates;
  - it reports more than 250 liaison offices and more than 2,000 liaison personnel, portal/TV advertising, nationwide prepaid-card distribution and direct web payment;
  - direct sales through UnionPay/China PnR incurred service fees below 1%, lower than distributor discounts/incentives;
  - the company frames `player first`, rapid adaptation to preferences and F2P virtual products/services as core competitive capabilities.
- Confidence: HIGH for filed financial/organizational facts; MEDIUM-HIGH for management's self-description of competitive advantage
- Boundary:
  - filings are company-authored and can frame strategy favorably;
  - IDC market estimates embedded in the F-1 are third-party market research reproduced by the issuer;
  - do not infer that every later Giant title had the same economics as ZT.

## Current evidence-level conclusions

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED

- ZT Online was an important Chinese F2P success but did not invent the F2P MMO model;
- free-to-play was already overtaking pay-to-play in China around 2006;
- Shi explicitly reasoned in terms of a large free population plus high-spending users;
- Giant built a large product/operations organization and a very large national acquisition/distribution perimeter;
- Shi transferred earlier consumer-market/ground-marketing capability into games;
- ZT generated very large audited revenue relative to most contemporary Chinese game-development projects;
- the company treated rapid updates and player preference adaptation as core production capabilities.

### SUPPORTED / NEEDS MORE GRANULARITY

- Shi's direct conversations with hundreds of players materially influenced product decisions;
- the county/township strategy was partly a result of his earlier rural-consumer research habits;
- the `salary` system functioned as an explicit cross-subsidy from high spenders toward population density/retention;
- the success of ZT materially accelerated industry imitation of high-depth virtual-item monetization.

### PARTIAL / NEXT VERIFY

- exact creative credit for core ZT systems across Shi, design leads and engineering leads;
- exact initial team formation from Shanda/other companies;
- reconciled development-budget figure (RMB20m initial investment vs RMB40m media R&D claim vs accounting capitalization);
- independent measurement of player geography and the claimed 70% lower-tier/rural share;
- exact share of ZT revenue attributable to whales / high spenders;
- how much later Chinese F2P/MMO design can causally be traced to ZT versus parallel Shanda/Korean precedents;
- player-harm / churn / spending-distribution data under stronger primary documentation;
- exact relationship between ZT's business-model success and later card/mobile monetization practices.
