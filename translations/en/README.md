This project asks how an unusual developer or unusually small team acquires the conditions needed to produce a game: accumulated capabilities, practical constraints, scope choices, organization, cash flow, market access, failure and chance.

It does not treat successful games as proof that hard work guarantees success. The research separates popular myths from evidence and examines support, failed predecessors and conditions that cannot easily be copied.

## Research structure

A Case tracks myth, origin, prior capabilities, runway, production, scope, failure, market access, environment, luck, verdict, transferable decisions and context-specific conditions. A Claim states a falsifiable proposition, its scope, supporting and opposing evidence, current status and limits on extrapolation.

Facts must trace to public sources. P0 means contemporaneous primary evidence; P1 means retrospective primary evidence; S1 and S2 distinguish stronger and weaker secondary sources; H marks our hypotheses. Unknown details remain UNKNOWN. A successful team's choices alone do not establish causation.

Research dossiers and finished narratives are separate. Reader-facing prose must point back to Case / Claim / Evidence, rather than fill gaps for dramatic effect. Private projects are excluded as cases, sources and application targets.
