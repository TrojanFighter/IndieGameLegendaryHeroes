# Play → Production / 第四次工业革命能力形成 Audit 001

- Status: PROVISIONAL RESEARCH NOTE
- Purpose: 为 `EDITORIAL-MISSION.md` 中“不能把游戏一概视为非生产性娱乐”的书级立场建立证据边界。
- Not a Claim: 当前不建立“玩游戏导致职业成功”的正式因果命题。

## 1. 要反驳的命题是什么

不反驳：

> 过度游戏可能伤害健康、睡眠、学习、工作与社会功能。

WHO 的 ICD-11 明确承认 gaming disorder；这部分风险必须保留。

真正要反驳的是更强、也更容易被事实否定的总命题：

> **数字游戏只是非生产性消费，与能力形成、职业技能和生产者身份没有结构性连接。**

只要存在稳定、可复现的 `player → modifier / creator → public work → professional` 路径，这个总命题就不能成立。

---

## 2. Health Boundary

### WHO — Gaming disorder

- Class: P0 / institutional health standard
- URL: https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gaming-disorder

WHO 将 gaming disorder 定义为失去控制、游戏优先级压倒其他活动、在负面后果下仍继续，并造成显著功能损害的行为模式；通常需持续约 12 个月才能满足诊断条件。

同一页面指出，这类障碍只影响数字游戏玩家中的一小部分。

### WHO — Addictive behaviour overview

- Class: P0 / institutional health guidance
- URL: https://www.who.int/health-topics/addictive-behaviour

WHO 的概括同时指出，大量人参与电子游戏，通常并不产生显著健康后果；特定模式才可能发展成损害性行为。

### Boundary

因此书中禁止两种对称错误：

- `gaming disorder exists → gaming as a whole is harmful`；
- `gaming can support learning/skills → excessive gaming is harmless`。

## 3. Fourth Industrial Revolution / Future-of-work context

### World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs Report 2025

- Class: S1 / large employer survey and labour-market report
- URL: https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/

The report identifies current/future skills including:

- analytical thinking;
- creative thinking;
- technological literacy;
- curiosity and lifelong learning;
- resilience, flexibility and agility;
- systems thinking;
- AI and big data;
- design and user experience;
- marketing and media.

It also projects substantial skill disruption through 2030 and emphasizes reskilling/upskilling needs.

### What this supports

It does **not** prove games teach these skills.

It supports the book's historical framing that future-work capability is increasingly broader than memorized domain knowledge or one fixed credential, making informal project-based learning and technological participation more economically relevant objects of study.

## 4. General cognitive evidence — keep claims modest

### 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis

- Source: `The association between video game play and cognitive ability: A systematic review and meta-analysis`
- Journal index page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S000169182600911X
- Coverage: 133 studies, 14,245 participants, studies published 2005–2025.

Reported result: statistically significant but generally small associations/effects across memory, spatial ability, visual attention, cognitive control and intelligence in the included designs.

### Boundary

This literature is **not** the central argument of this book.

We should not reduce the defence of games to “games raise IQ/reaction speed.” Even a small cognitive effect does not establish career formation, and genre/self-selection/confounding remain relevant.

The stronger contribution of this corpus is historical/production evidence for **identity and capability conversion**.

# 6. Conversion Ladder — working model

This is a descriptive framework, not a universal developmental ladder.

### P0 — Consumption

Play / entertainment / rest.

No productivity claim required.

### P1 — Comparative literacy

Player can compare rules, genres, loops, feedback and design choices.

Potential output: taste / criticism / selection ability.

### P2 — Modification

Maps, mods, servers, ROM hacks, configs, balance changes, UGC.

The player first alters an existing production system.

### P3 — Independent artifact

Prototype, jam game, tool, mod pack, map pack, video, guide, plugin, server/community system.

The learner now has an external artifact rather than only knowledge.

### P4 — Public feedback / reputation

Unknown users interact with the artifact; creator receives criticism, usage data, community response or peer recognition.

### P5 — Market / organization

Artifact produces income, paid work, recruitment, collaborators, clients or a company.

### Research rule

Never infer P5 from P0.

The book's evidence value lies in documenting the intermediate transitions.

---

# 7. Why this matters for goal education

Standardized education often assumes:

```text
choose goal
→ acquire credential
→ enter occupation
```

Many digital-production biographies in this corpus instead look like:

```text
interest
→ repeated voluntary attention
→ small artifact
→ feedback
→ stronger capability
→ new opportunity
→ revised goal
→ larger commitment
```

This does not prove one model should replace the other.

It does show that **goal formation itself can be iterative and evidence-driven**, which is a central reader-layer theme.

---

# 8. Failure and Harm must stay inside the model

Future audits should also collect:

- high playtime with no capability conversion;
- creator/modder burnout;
- exploitative UGC labour and platform dependence;
- gambling-like monetization exposure;
- parasocial/community toxicity;
- sleep/health impairment;
- young creators whose market income is unstable;
- people who gained strong game-production skills but failed to find sustainable work.

This prevents the book from replacing “games are harmful” with an equally crude “games are educational” ideology.

---

## Preliminary Verdict

The defensible fourth-industrial-revolution argument is not:

> **Playing games makes people successful.**

It is:

> **Digital games can function as complex cultural/technical environments in which some users move from consumption into comparison, modification, tool use, public creation, collaboration and professional production. Treating all gaming time as categorically opposed to learning therefore uses an obsolete model of how digital capabilities can form.**

The magnitude, probability and distribution of that conversion remain empirical questions and must be audited case by case.
